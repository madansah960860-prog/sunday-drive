About Sunday Drive Goods

Two people, fifteen products, and a firm rule about never implying anything is old.

Who we are

Sunday Drive Mercantile LLC trades as Sunday Drive Goods from a unit on Cottonwood Street in Tucson, Arizona. Two of us run it. We pack the orders, we answer the telephone, and one of us fixes the things that come back.

We are a retailer. Everything here is bought in from manufacturers and importers. We do not make anything and we are not going to imply a workshop we do not have.

The one thing to be clear about

Nothing on this site is an antique, a collectible, or an original of anything. Every item is newly manufactured. Where a piece is copied from a period design — the milk can, the French-script bowl, the Roman-numeral clock — the product page says "styled after" or "newly made to that pattern" in the first paragraph, and the photograph caption says the same thing.

We are strict about this for a plain reason. A shop selling reproduction goods in a nostalgic voice is one careless sentence away from implying that a new item is old, and an implied claim is still a claim. It is also a Google Ads misrepresentation issue, and it is simply dishonest. So we say it on the product page, in the footer of every page, and here.

We also use no period manufacturer's name, no brand mark that is not ours, and no "as seen in" anything.

What gets on the shelf

Fifteen products. Something earns a place by passing three tests.

  1. Does it work without a battery, or does the battery genuinely earn its place? The kitchen timer and the alarm clock are wind-up. The wall clock takes one AA and runs eighteen months on it. Neither of them needs an app, a pairing sequence or a firmware update.
  2. Can it be repaired, or at least maintained? The record player's stylus is a common replaceable part. The chess set's pieces are standard Staunton. Enamelware can be scrubbed for thirty years. We are not interested in stocking something that becomes rubbish the moment one part of it fails.
  3. Can we describe the catch? Everything has one. Enamel chips. Styrene scratches. Mechanical clocks tick. Ceramic cartridges track heavy. If the honest description of a product's weakness would stop us selling it, it does not go on the shelf — but if it is a fair trade, we write it down and let you decide.

Where orders ship from

Every parcel is packed and posted from Tucson, Arizona. We ship within the United States only — all fifty states, the District of Columbia and Puerto Rico — and we ship to PO boxes and to APO, FPO and DPO addresses, with two size exceptions noted on the shipping policy page.

Most orders leave within one to two business days. The suitcase record player takes two to three, because it is double-boxed.

What we will not claim

- No reception claims. We will tell you the radio's bands, its aerial and its speaker size. We will not tell you how many miles it will pull a station from, because that depends on your house, your terrain and the transmitter, not on our radio. It is not a weather-alert radio and has no NOAA function, and we say so on the page. - No health claims of any kind. Nothing here is therapeutic, nothing improves anything about you, and the word "wellness" does not appear on this site. - No safety guarantees. Enamelware is sold with its actual heat ratings and its actual limits. We will not tell you anything is "safe", only what it is rated for. - No "lasts forever" and no "non-toxic". Both are claims we cannot substantiate on every item, so we make neither. - No country-of-origin claims. We do not print "Made in USA" on anything, because we do not hold documentation meeting the FTC's "all or virtually all" standard. We can tell you where a parcel ships from, which is a different thing. - No reviews, no ratings, no urgency. No stars, no testimonials, no countdown clocks, no "only two left", no crossed-out prices we never charged.

Accessibility commitment

This site is built to the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines version 2.1 at Level AA and tested at that standard.

The display face is a condensed uppercase type, used only for headings, buttons and labels — never for running text, because condensed capitals are hard to read in quantity. Body copy is set at 18 pixels in a plain sans face with a 1.65 line height. Text contrast is at least 4.5 to 1 everywhere and better than 7 to 1 for body copy; the mustard accent is used as a background behind near-black text, never as light text on a light ground. Every control is a real button or link with a visible focus outline and a target at least 48 pixels tall. A skip-to-content link is first in the tab order. Every image has meaningful alternative text and explicit width and height. The layout reflows to 320 pixels without horizontal scrolling and works at 200 per cent zoom.

No part of this site animates by itself. There are no rotating banners, nothing appears over the page when it loads, and pages end rather than scrolling forever.
