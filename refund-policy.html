Sixty days, and why

You have 60 days from delivery to send something back. Changing your mind is a perfectly good reason. It has to be unused and in its original box.

Sixty is longer than almost anybody gives, and it is deliberate. Half of what we sell is bought as a present, often two months before the occasion, and a thirty-day window expires while the thing is still in a cupboard waiting to be wrapped. A present bought in October and opened at Christmas is still inside our window.

Where the fault is ours we pay the postage both ways and refund everything including your original shipping. Where you simply changed your mind, you pay the return postage and we refund the goods. No restocking fee, ever.

When the clock starts

The 60 days run from the carrier's delivery date, not the order date and not the day the recipient opened it. Your request must reach us inside those 60 days; the parcel then has a further 14 days to get back to us.

What condition it has to be in

- Unused. Not wound in and run, not brewed in, not hung on a wall, not played with over a weekend. - Complete. Every piece, both winding keys, the spare stylus, the spare queens, all six mats, the hanging template. - In its original box, with the inner packing. - Undamaged by you. Looking at it and putting it back is expected. A dented enamel mug is not.

The outer shipping carton does not have to be pristine.

If something arrives back used or incomplete we photograph it, email you, and offer either a partial refund reflecting the condition or the item back to you at our expense. We do not keep it and refuse the refund.

What we cannot take back

- Playing cards with a broken cellophane seal. A deck that has been opened cannot be sold as new to anyone else. Sealed decks are fully returnable. - A record player whose stylus has been used. Stylus wear is not reversible. If you have played records on it, it is used. The spare stylus in the box does not change that. - Items marked final sale. There are none at present. If there ever are, the product page will say so in plain words above the Add to Cart button.

None of that applies to anything faulty. A radio that does not tune, a clock that will not hold time, a chipped mug out of the box, a cracked pane in a frame set — all returnable however far in you are.

Who pays the postage

We pay, and we refund your original shipping too :: It arrived damaged or chipped. It is faulty or fails in normal use inside the window. We sent the wrong thing. The item is materially different from its description here. You pay, and we refund the price of the goods :: You changed your mind. It is heavier or larger than you pictured and the dimensions were correctly stated. The alarm clock ticks more than you expected, having read that it ticks. You bought two and kept one.

A change-of-mind return typically runs $8 to $14 for small items and $25 to $45 for the record player or the frame sets, paid by you directly to the carrier. We take no cut and we do not mark it up.

If your order qualified for free shipping over $65 and returning part of it drops the kept portion below $65, we will not retrospectively charge you the $8.95.

Exchanges

We do not run a formal exchange. Place a new order for what you want and return the first under this policy; that way the replacement is already travelling while the original comes back.

If the exchange is needed because we sent the wrong thing, none of that applies — the right item goes out at once and you pay nothing.

Faults, and what is not a fault

Report transit damage within 14 days of delivery with photographs. For a fault in ordinary use, tell us as soon as it shows and within the 60-day window. We ask what happened, look at photographs, and replace or refund at our cost where it is a manufacturing fault.

These are not faults, and it is fairer to list them than to argue about them later:

  • Enamel chipped after delivery. Enamel is glass fused to steel; it chips when knocked. Every enamel page says so.
  • Rust from a chip left wet. Same reason, and the care instructions say to dry it.
  • A mechanical clock that ticks. Both wind-up pieces tick audibly. There is no silent mechanical movement and the product pages say so.
  • A wind-up timer that is inaccurate under three minutes. A property of the escapement, stated on the page, with the workaround given.
  • Styrene glazing scratched by dry wiping. The rustic frame set is glazed in styrene, not glass, and the page says to dust rather than wipe.
  • A stylus worn out after long use. A consumable, replaceable, with a spare in the box.
  • Weak radio reception at your address. We make no reception claim anywhere on this site precisely so that this is clear in advance.

Cancelling

Before it ships, telephone (520) 555-0183 or email howdy@sundaydrivegoods.com with the order number. We cancel and refund in full within 7 working days and we do not ask why.

After a tracking number exists it becomes a return. You can refuse delivery — it comes back and we refund the goods — but refused parcels take two or three weeks to find their way home, so an email is faster.

If we cannot sort it out

Email howdy@sundaydrivegoods.com with Escalation in the subject line and your order number. One of the two owners reads it personally and replies within two business days.

Nothing here limits any right you have under Arizona consumer law or the consumer protection law of your own state.
