The detail

About this one

This is the shape a milk can has had for a hundred years: a tapered body, a stepped shoulder, a press-fit lid and a wooden grip across a wire bail. Ours is newly made in porcelain enamel over steel, in the same pattern.

It holds 2 quarts, which is 64 fluid ounces. Most people do not use it for milk. It is a very good vessel for a jug of iced tea on a table, for utensils next to a stove, for a bunch of cut flowers that would flop in a wide vase, or for dry goods with the lid on.

The handle is a turned beech grip on a galvanised wire bail, so the whole thing carries comfortably in one hand when it is full — about 5.6 pounds. The lid is a press fit, not a seal: it keeps dust out and it will not keep liquid in if the can tips over.

Not sealed, not pressurised, not for hot liquids left standing. Enamel on steel, so it chips if dropped and a chip to bare steel will rust.
