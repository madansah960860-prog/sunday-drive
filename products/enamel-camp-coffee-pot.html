About this one

A percolator makes coffee the way it was made before anybody owned a machine: water boils, climbs the tube, falls through the grounds, and goes round again until it is as strong as you let it get. It takes eight or nine minutes and you can hear when it is going.

The pot is porcelain enamel on heavy-gauge steel, 48 fluid ounces to the fill line, which is eight 6-ounce cups or about four mugs. The internal basket and stem are stainless, not aluminium, and they lift out as one piece so it can be used as a plain kettle. There is a glass knob in the lid so you can watch the colour come up.

It works on gas, electric and induction hobs, on a camp burner, and directly over a fire — the whole point of enamel on steel. The handle is the same steel, so **it gets hot**: use a cloth, and there is no plastic on it to melt if you leave it too near a flame.

Enamel is glass fused to steel. It will chip if you drop it or bang it on a stone, and a chip that reaches the steel will eventually rust. That is the honest limit of the material and it is why these are cheap to replace and last decades if you are careful.
