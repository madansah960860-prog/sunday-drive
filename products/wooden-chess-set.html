The detail

About this one

The board is the storage, which is the whole idea. It is 15 inches square, hinged down the middle, and folds into a 15 by 7.5 inch case with a brass clasp; the pieces travel inside in a fitted felt tray, so there is no separate bag to lose.

Squares are 1.5 inches, which suits a king of 3 inches — the proportion tournament sets use, and the reason the board does not look crowded when the pieces are out. The pieces are a standard Staunton pattern in stained and natural hardwood, weighted with a steel disc in the base and felted underneath so they sit where you put them.

There is an extra queen of each colour in the tray, because promoting a pawn and having to use an upside-down rook is the small indignity of cheap sets.

The board face is a printed and lacquered veneer rather than inlaid squares, which is what keeps this at $46 rather than $200. We would rather say so than let you find out.
