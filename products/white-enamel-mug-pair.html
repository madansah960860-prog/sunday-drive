The detail

About this one

Enamel mugs do one thing no ceramic mug does: they survive being dropped on a tiled floor. They dent, and they will eventually chip, but they do not shatter, which is why they have been the kitchen and camp mug of choice for a century.

These are 12 fluid ounces to the brim, 3.1 inches across and 3.3 tall, in porcelain enamel over steel with a rolled navy rim. The rolled rim matters twice over: it is comfortable against the lip, and it is the edge most likely to chip on a cheaper mug with a raw enamel edge.

They stack, which is the other reason to own enamel — two of these take barely more shelf room than one. The handle is welded, not riveted, so there is no rivet head on the inside collecting coffee.

Two honest caveats. Enamel over steel conducts heat: a mug of fresh coffee is hot to hold for the first minute, and the handle stays cool but the body does not. And they are metal, so they are not microwave safe. Everything else — dishwasher, freezer, oven to 500°F, camp fire — is fine.
