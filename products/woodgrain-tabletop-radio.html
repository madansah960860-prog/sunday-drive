The detail

About this one

A kitchen radio should turn on with a knob and get loud enough to hear over a tap running. This one does both. The cabinet is a real walnut-look wood veneer over MDF rather than printed plastic, which is why it has the weight it has and why it sounds the way it does — a wood box is simply a better speaker enclosure than a hollow plastic one.

Two knobs. The left one is volume and the on-off switch in the same motion; the right is the tuning dial, geared down about 4 to 1 so you can actually land on a station instead of skating past it. The dial face is backlit in warm amber and there is a band switch between them for AM and FM.

Inside is a 4-inch full-range driver, 3 watts RMS, with a bass port at the back. It runs off mains power with a 5-foot cord and there is a 3.5mm headphone socket on the back that cuts the speaker, plus a 3.5mm auxiliary input if you want to play something through it.

**Reception depends entirely on where you are.** FM uses a 30-inch wire aerial that trails out of the back; AM uses an internal ferrite bar, which means you tune AM partly by turning the whole radio. We are not going to quote you a range in miles, because any shop that does is guessing about your house. This is not a weather-alert radio and it has no NOAA function.
