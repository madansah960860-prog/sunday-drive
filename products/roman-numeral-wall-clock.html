The detail

About this one

Fourteen inches is the size that reads from across a room without dominating a wall. The face is printed to look like aged paper under glass, with Roman numerals about 1.3 inches tall and a fine minute track, in a metal case with an antiqued bronze finish.

It is a **silent sweep** movement, which is the thing to check before buying any wall clock. A standard quartz movement steps once a second and ticks audibly; a sweep movement moves the second hand continuously and makes no sound at all. In a kitchen nobody notices a tick. In a bedroom or a study it is the difference between keeping a clock and taking it down.

One AA battery, not included, runs it for about eighteen months. There is a single keyhole on the back and it hangs on one screw or one picture hook.

Roman numerals take a moment longer to read than Arabic ones — that is simply true, and if reading a clock at a glance from the doorway matters more than the look of it, a large-numeral Arabic face is the better choice.

  • Looking after it

    • Wipe the glass with a dry cloth. Do not spray cleaner directly at it — it will run behind the lens.
    • Use an alkaline AA battery. Remove it if the clock is stored for a long period.
    • Hang it on a fixing rated for at least 5 lb; 2.4 lb of clock on a tired picture hook is how clocks end up on the floor.
    • Keep it out of a steamy bathroom; condensation gets behind the lens and marks the face.
